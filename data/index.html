The Shape of the Spreader Graph

We clustered 807 conspiracy-positive sources by breadth, political lean, topic and platform — and eight archetypes fall out. A tiny video/social mega-spreader tier bridges dozens of theories (Alex Jones spans 31) while a long podcast tail stays narrow; political lean is predicted by topic, not loudness; pseudoscience is its own apolitical lane; and deep-state/QAnon is the connective tissue, 27% of all source-to-theory links.

Theory dataset

One row per tracked conspiracy theory (192 total). The flat, spreadsheet-ready cut of the database.

Includes:
  • Political lean (centroid) + bimodality / modality
  • Power, threat, and harm scores; lifecycle status
  • Ontology axes: family, mechanism, arena, controlling interest, infrastructure burden
  • Structural pattern (archetype) + sub-theory parent
  • Core claims, summary, activity metrics & dates
Omitted:
  • Voice-of-the-Believer / Voice-of-Reason narrative prose
  • Individual atoms (post-level data)
  • Data-visualisation arrays

SCTO ontology

The Structured Conspiracy-Theory Ontology in RDF/Turtle (OWL). Importable into Protégé, GraphDB, or any SPARQL endpoint.

Includes:
  • Typed vocabulary classes (family, mechanism, arena, claim & evidence types…)
  • Per-theory axes, claims, evidence, kernels-of-truth + their relationships
  • Structural patterns (archetypes) via hasPattern
  • Sub-theory hierarchy via subTheoryOf
