How this material was gathered

Pipeline (LEM-ES-MIA lane): each station's public live stream is captured in scheduled ~12-minute windows during talk programming (ffmpeg). The Spanish audio is transcribed with faster-whisper (Spanish ASR, run locally), machine-translated to English (local qwen2.5:14b), and the translation is analyzed by the same Conspiracy Today analyzer that scans SignalWatch's other sources — a translate-then-classify design, so Spanish-language content is judged by the identical narrative criteria as English content. Narrative cards additionally carry: similar-claim grouping; matches against the tracked-theory corpus (claims this lane itself contributed); matches against the breaking-news events the event-probe lane tracks; and rhetoric flags (dehumanization, scapegoating, calls to action, fear appeals, institution delegitimization) assigned by the local analyzer only when clearly supported by the transcript.
Limitations, honestly: capture windows are samples, not continuous monitoring, and talk radio carries heavy ad blocks — some windows are mostly advertising. 3 of 42 capture windows produced empty or too-thin transcripts (stream or transcription failure) and are excluded; we found that the analyzer can hallucinate generic conspiracy titles when handed an empty transcript, so every narrative on this page is additionally required to be grounded in its window's transcript text — and any representative quote that cannot be located in its transcript is discarded. ASR and machine translation introduce their own errors; quotes are translated excerpts, not verbatim English speech. Rhetoric flags are structural indicators from a local model, not verdicts. A narrative appearing on a program documents what went to air in that window — it is not a claim about a station's editorial position, and never a verdict about any individual.
