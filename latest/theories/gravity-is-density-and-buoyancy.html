Voice of Reason

The theory under examination holds that gravity, as described in mainstream physics, does not exist as a real attractive force; that falling and sinking behavior is fully explained by density and buoyancy acting within a surrounding medium; that Einstein's general relativity is a mathematical fiction propped up by institutional investment rather than empirical success; and that electromagnetic and plasma forces can account for every phenomenon — orbital mechanics, light deflection, large-scale cosmic structure — that physics currently attributes to gravity. The gravitational wave detection GW170817 is reframed as a real signal misread through a question-begging relativistic lens.

The central problem with the density-and-buoyancy substitution is that it is not an alternative explanation — it is a circular non-answer that quietly smuggles gravity back in. Archimedes' principle states that the buoyant force on a submerged object equals the weight of the displaced fluid, and the standard formula for buoyancy is B = −ρgV, where g is gravitational acceleration. Remove g from the equation and buoyancy vanishes entirely. Buoyancy happens because a fluid has a pressure gradient, and that pressure gradient exists because the fluid is subjected to a gravitational acceleration. The theory therefore does not explain falling objects by replacing gravity; it explains them by assuming gravity while pretending to have done away with it. This is not a minor technicality — it is a foundational self-contradiction. There is also a fatal directional problem. Gravity is a force and therefore a vector — it points in a direction. Density and buoyancy are scalars. There is nothing in the density-buoyancy framework to explain why motion is downward; there is no mathematical expression for the force. A "denser object sinks" statement tells you the outcome only if you already know which way is "down," and down is defined entirely by the gravitational field the theory denies. A decisive experimental test has been reproducible since Galileo and is now performable in any physics classroom: in a perfect vacuum, a feather and a bowling ball dropped simultaneously from the same height hit the ground at the same time, because gravity accelerates all objects equally regardless of mass, and the vacuum eliminates air resistance. If density and buoyancy governed the outcome, denser objects would fall faster in any medium, including a vacuum where there is no medium at all. They do not. Apollo 15 astronaut David Scott demonstrated the same result on the Moon — a falcon feather and a hammer fell at identical speed — without the encumbrance of a vacuum chamber. No density-buoyancy model predicted or can explain these results.

The electromagnetic and plasma-universe substitution for gravity fails with equal clarity when subjected to the standard test of a scientific theory: prediction. In the electric-universe model the Sun is lit by electrically excited plasma, which predicts it should produce no neutrinos, while the standard fusion model predicts copious neutrino production. The EU model clearly fails this test — solar neutrinos have not only been observed but used to image the Sun's interior. Specific EU claims about electric comets and planetary scarring have been further falsified by spacecraft missions like Rosetta, which found no evidence of the predicted mega-lightning, finding instead gravitational and chemical explanations. The EU community's response to these failures is revealing: when skeptic Michael Shermer asked for peer-reviewed papers at an Electric Universe conference, a leading proponent responded, "In an interdisciplinary science like the Electric Universe, you could say we have no peers, so peer review is not available." This is the signature of an unfalsifiable position: when a theory can explain any result after the fact, has no mechanism for disconfirmation, and refuses engagement with the community of experts best positioned to check its claims, it has exited the domain of empirical science. One hallmark of a robust scientific theory is the ability to make accurate, testable predictions; critics of the Electric Universe contend it lacks the predictive power demonstrated by mainstream astrophysical models.

The claim that GW170817 was circularly interpreted deserves direct engagement, because the event is real and the data are public. On August 17, 2017, the Advanced LIGO and Virgo detectors observed GW170817 with a combined signal-to-noise ratio of 32.4 and a false-alarm rate of less than one per 80,000 years. Crucially, the event was not confirmed by LIGO alone. The Fermi Gamma-ray Burst Monitor and the INTEGRAL spacecraft independently detected a gamma-ray burst, GRB 170817A, at the same time and from the same location. The probability of that near-simultaneous spatial and temporal coincidence occurring by chance is 5.0 × 10⁻⁸. Optical and infrared observations independently established that GW170817 was a neutron star merger, entirely separate from the gravitational wave data. The observed time delay between the gravitational wave and the gamma-ray burst was used to constrain the difference between the speed of gravity and the speed of light to within a factor of 10⁻¹⁵, a precision that devastates virtually every proposed alternative to general relativity and was derived from the data itself, not assumed in advance. The charge that the interpretation "presupposes" relativity ignores the fact that every single independent instrument — optical, infrared, radio, X-ray, and gamma-ray telescopes on separate spacecraft and ground arrays — converged on the same conclusion through completely different physical mechanisms.

There is a legitimate observation at the seed of this theory: physics does acknowledge that gravity's mechanism is not fully unified with quantum mechanics, and that the deep ontological nature of curved spacetime raises genuine interpretive questions philosophers and physicists actively debate. Curiosity about foundational physics is healthy. What the density-buoyancy theory does with that legitimate uncertainty, however, is to perform a classic conspiracy-theory maneuver: it treats any open scientific question as evidence that the entire edifice is fraudulent, and then substitutes a model that is not only unverified but demonstrably self-contradictory — one that requires the very force it denies in order to function at all. Researchers and journalists who have studied the flat-earth and alternative-physics ecosystem document how the movement's adhesive is distrust of mainstream expertise rather than any technical consensus among its own proponents, which is why multiple competing and mutually incompatible "replacements" for gravity coexist within the same community without anyone noticing the problem. The harm this class of theory produces is primarily epistemic: it trains its adherents to treat the insistence on mathematical rigor and experimental replication as signs of corruption rather than as the tools by which errors are actually caught, making every well-tested finding look like just another layer of the cover-up. That circular immunization against evidence is not a feature of the theory — it is its entire load-bearing structure.
