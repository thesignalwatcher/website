Voice of Reason

mRNA vaccines work by delivering temporary instructions that prompt cells to produce a protein, triggering an immune response. The mRNA itself degrades within days and never enters the cell nucleus where DNA is stored, so the mechanism for permanent genetic alteration simply isn't there. The Bayer executive quote is real, but in context he was describing a marketing challenge — that the public might not have accepted mRNA technology if it had been framed as cell and gene therapy from the start — not making a scientific claim that it is gene therapy under regulatory definitions.

The leap happens when a candid remark about public perception gets reread as an admission of deliberate mislabeling. When a framework treats missing proof as proof of concealment, almost any ambiguous detail fits. The simpler read: an executive used imprecise language about a novel platform, and mRNA vaccines cleared a compressed but real review process. Whether that review was sufficient is a fair and open question.
