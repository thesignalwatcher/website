DNC Rigged the 2016 Primary Against Bernie

Threat · ElevatedFringePower 19

The Democratic National Committee (DNC) openly sabotaged Bernie Sanders in the 2016 primary, denying him a fair shot at the nomination.

Overview

Core claims

  • The DNC's conduct in the 2016 primary was illegal.
  • Vote totals were secretly manipulated and the actual outcome of the 2016 primary was rigged.
  • Superdelegates overrode the will of the voters to hand Clinton the nomination.
  • Absent the DNC's interference, Sanders would have won the nomination.

Voice of the Believer

Bernie Sanders represented an anti-establishment flavor within the Democratic Party that the party's own leadership could not tolerate, and they made sure he never had a real shot. The DNC was conspiring to put Hillary Clinton at the top spot even before the primaries — Clinton and DNC collusion began in 2014, long before a single vote was cast. Debbie Wasserman Schultz's role as campaign co-chair for Clinton going back years tells you everything you need to know about why she was running the DNC the way she was. The DNC was being run by Hillary Clinton's headquarters during the primaries, full stop. When the Wikileaks releases dropped, that wasn't speculation anymore — the DNC email hack revealed reprehensible and indefensible internal communications showing exactly what was happening. DNC officials were not neutral during the primary process, attempting to undermine Bernie Sanders' campaign and reflecting nothing but contempt for his voters. They were mocking him for wanting a debate in California. They were going after his character, including speculating about his religion and attempting to smear him as an atheist in Southern states. Clinton campaign emails suggest rigging the primary process was not some rogue operation — it ran straight through the top of the party apparatus.

Then Donna Brazile came out and confirmed what everyone already knew. Donna Brazile says Clinton campaign effectively controlled DNC finances, messaging, and strategy before the nomination was even decided. She admits the DNC rigged the primary against Bernie Sanders. This isn't a fringe reading — this is the former chair of the Democratic National Committee saying out loud that the institution was captured. The Democratic Establishment stole the nomination from Bernie, and when Bernie Sanders was cheated out of the DNC in 2016, he endorsed Hillary Clinton anyway, which is the only reason this story didn't blow the party apart entirely.

No other primary in U.S. history was as rigged as the one in 2016. Superdelegates were pledged to Clinton before Iowa. The debate schedule was engineered to limit Sanders' exposure. The DNC's actions were unethical and unfair, making Hillary Clinton's nomination questionable on its face. Bernie Sanders would have won if not for DNC interference — the polling, the enthusiasm, the grassroots fundraising all pointed the same direction. The machine overrode the will of actual Democratic voters, handed the nomination to a deeply compromised candidate, and then acted surprised when they lost in November. The system is rigged, and 2016 proved it.

Voice of Reason

The DNC emails released in 2016 did show some staffers expressing frustration with Sanders and, in a few cases, floating ideas to undermine him — that is real, it was embarrassing, and several officials resigned over it. What those emails did not show was any coordinated scheme that altered vote counts or changed the outcome. Sanders lost the pledged-delegate race to Clinton by roughly 360 delegates across millions of votes in dozens of contests. Superdelegates were never formally counted until after the convention, so their stated preferences did not override voters — they ratified the pledged-delegate result.

The leap from "some staffers were biased" to "the whole primary was stolen" requires reading staff contempt as proof of a hidden machine capable of manipulating millions of ballots with no forensic trace. Once you accept that machine as real, every gap in the record — every unreleased email, every unexplained result — feels like more evidence. But a feeling of fit is not the same as finding the mechanism. The simpler read is that an insurgent candidate who entered the race late, with lower name recognition and weaker support among Black and Southern Democratic voters, lost a competitive primary. Could bias in party infrastructure have cost him some margin at the edges?
