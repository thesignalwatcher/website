Voice of Reason

Vortex-Based Mathematics, developed by Marko Rodin and promoted by his associate Randy Powell, asserts that the repeating sequence of digital roots produced by successive doublings — 1, 2, 4, 8, 7, 5 — along with the separate behavior of 3, 6, and 9, reveals the hidden mathematical architecture of the universe, and that a coil of wire wound according to this pattern on a toroidal core produces anomalous electromagnetic effects, including so-called "over-unity" free energy that established physics cannot account for.

The foundational mathematical claim collapses the moment you examine what the 1-2-4-8-7-5 pattern actually is. Vortex math operates by reducing numbers to their digital root — repeatedly summing a number's digits until a single digit is obtained. The digital roots of successive powers of 2 do indeed progress in the sequence 1, 2, 4, 8, 7, 5, 1 — cycling indefinitely. But this is not a cosmic secret: the digital root of any number is equivalent to that number modulo 9, with the special case that numbers divisible by 9 have a digital root of 9. The 1-2-4-8-7-5 pattern is therefore a direct, provable consequence of the fact that 2 is a generator of a cyclic subgroup of the integers modulo 9 — a result that falls out of standard modular arithmetic covered in undergraduate number theory. The pattern exists entirely because humans chose to count in base 10. The sequence only works in base 10, as it is a result of the interaction of doubling with the base, and the same pattern does not appear in any other number system. Rodin's diagram would look entirely different — and generate entirely different "mystical" sequences — in base 8, base 12, or base 60. Base 10 is an entirely human construction, and so if the diagram is "divine," then so too are all the uncountably infinite beautiful diagrams that arise from other bases — diagrams that vortex math proponents simply ignore because they are committed to the number ten. The fact that 3, 6, and 9 fall outside the doubling cycle is not a sign of their elevated metaphysical status; it is because 3 and 9 are factors of 9, making their digital-root behavior under multiplication by 2 trivially predictable. Rodin himself attributes his interest in these specific numbers to converting a Bahá'í religious phrase into numerals using the Abjad system, which happened to sum to 9 — a number considered holy in that tradition. The mathematical pattern was found because Rodin was looking for it within a pre-selected religious framework, a classic case of motivated numerology rather than discovery.

The engineering claim — that the Rodin coil produces over-unity electromagnetic effects — fails at an even more fundamental level. The Rodin coil is a specific way of winding copper wire around a toroidal core, and while proponents argue it produces unusual electromagnetic properties suggesting breakthroughs in energy efficiency, no independent peer-reviewed studies have confirmed such results. Over-unity energy — producing more energy output than input — would require violating the first law of thermodynamics. The first law states that energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another; a perpetual motion machine would have to produce work without energy input. A violation of any of these laws would require exceptional proof to be considered credible. No such proof exists for the Rodin coil in any venue that subjects claims to independent replication. Claims that the coil generates energy are in the realm of perpetual motion or over-unity devices and are bogus; large transformers and motors already exceed 90% efficiency, leaving little room for the kind of revolutionary gain proponents advertise. The claim that mainstream physics "ignores" vortex math because it is threatening inverts the actual situation: physics ignores it because the coil's extraordinary claims have not been submitted to, much less survived, peer review. To critics, Rodin is a fringe inventor whose claims lack scientific validation.

The theory does tap into a legitimate and widespread frustration: the sense that established institutions are closed to heterodox thinking, and that corporate and academic gatekeeping prevents revolutionary energy solutions from reaching the public. Energy poverty is real, and large institutions do sometimes resist novel ideas. But the theory exploits this valid grievance by treating the absence of institutional acceptance as evidence of suppression rather than what the record actually shows — a complete absence of reproducible experimental evidence. The work gained traction online in the early 2000s, spreading through YouTube lectures, alternative energy sites, and New Age communities drawn to ideas of sacred geometry, free energy, and hidden universal laws. This distribution pathway is structurally significant: the claims bypass peer review by going directly to audiences primed to distrust it. The result is a theory that is unfalsifiable by design — every failed replication becomes proof of suppression, and every criticism confirms the conspiracy. Believers are invited to invest time, money, and credibility in winding coils and running tests that, when they inevitably produce no anomalous output, are attributed to impure materials, incorrect geometry, or bad faith — never to the possibility that the underlying premise is wrong. The concrete harm is that people divert real resources toward unworkable devices, and that a numerological framework dressed in the vocabulary of physics — "flux fields," "toroidal flow," "dimensional vectors" — erodes the public's ability to distinguish between genuine mathematical elegance and the appearance of it.
