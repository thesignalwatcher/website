Voice of Reason

Venezuela did develop its own domestic voting technology, and Hugo Chávez's government used the state-linked company Smartmatic to run elections there. That much is real. What's worth examining is the leap from that fact to the claim that this same system was secretly transplanted into U.S. election infrastructure to flip a presidential race. Smartmatic itself has stated it provided software in only one U.S. county in 2020 — Los Angeles — and not in any of the contested swing states central to this story. The claim skips past that entirely.

The story works by treating proximity as proof: Venezuelan-linked tech existed, U.S. elections used some technology, therefore the connection must be there. Once you accept a hidden network of foreign operatives and complicit domestic actors as a given, every ambiguous detail starts to look like confirmation — but that feeling of pieces fitting together is produced by the assumption, not by evidence of it. A claim that vast requires either evidence of coordination across thousands of election workers in multiple states, or the premise that all of them stayed silent. The simpler question worth sitting with: if this system could alter tallies undetectably, why did dozens of post-election audits, hand recounts, and court reviews — many overseen by Republicans — find nothing?

Origins & History

The claim that voting software was "created in Venezuela at the direction of Hugo Chavez" to manipulate elections emerged in November 2020 in the immediate aftermath of the U.S. presidential election. Rudy Giuliani raised related allegations on Fox News around November 15, 2020, and Sidney Powell amplified the specific Chavez framing at a press conference on November 19, 2020. The claims targeted both Dominion Voting Systems and Smartmatic, drawing on Smartmatic's documented history of operating in Venezuela — a real but, according to contemporaneous reporting, unrelated backstory — while conflating it with Dominion, a separate Canadian-founded company.

The allegations spread rapidly through right-wing media, with amplification from Lou Dobbs on Fox Business, Powell's legal filings (referred to colloquially as the "Kraken" suits), and broader conservative media outlets. Google Trends data reflects a significant spike in related search interest in November 2020.

FactCheck.org documented the claims shortly after they surfaced, noting among other things that Hugo Chavez died in 2013, years before the systems in question were used in the manner alleged ([FactCheck.org](https://www.factcheck.org/2020/11/baseless-conspiracy-theory-targets-another-election-technology-company/)). The precise origin of the Chavez narrative prior to Giuliani and Powell's public statements is not well documented in the available corpus.
