Voice of Reason

Fast food companies do engineer their products for repeat appeal — salt, fat, and sugar combinations that taste good and leave you wanting more. That part is real, documented, and openly discussed in food science. The companies profit from it and have every reason to keep refining it. None of that needs to be hidden.

The leap happens when deliberate palatability becomes deliberate mind control. Designing food to taste good is not the same as dosing it with secret behavioral chemicals — and no credible food-safety analysis has found such compounds. The claim feels true because it names a real fear (that powerful companies don't care about your health) and then adds a sinister motive that makes every salty, sweet craving seem like proof of manipulation. Once you assume hidden bad actors, the evidence you'd expect from ordinary profit-seeking looks identical to the evidence of a scheme — so nothing can rule the scheme out. That is the feeling of a pattern locking into place, not evidence that it has. The simpler read: processed food is engineered to sell, and that alone raises legitimate questions about nutrition and marketing — questions worth asking without requiring a secret ingredient list.
