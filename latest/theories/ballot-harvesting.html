Voice of Reason

Ballot collection by third parties — often called 'ballot harvesting' — is a real and legal practice in most U.S. states, and concerns about its potential for abuse are not invented. Isolated prosecuted cases of absentee ballot fraud do exist, typically involving individuals, not coordinated party machines. Regulators and courts in multiple states have reviewed these practices and found no systemic evidence of organized ballot manipulation tied to either party.

The leap happens at the word 'systematically': real instances of individual misconduct get reframed as proof of a hidden, party-wide infrastructure. Once you accept that hidden machine, every unverified anecdote — a name-dropped operative, a secondhand account — feels like another tile in the mosaic. That feeling is the pattern working on you, not evidence accumulating. The claim also needs you to believe that thousands of participants across many states have maintained operational secrecy — and that an absence of prosecutions proves cover-up rather than absence of crime. A simpler read: legal ballot collection exists, rare fraud exists, and the two are being merged into something the evidence does not yet support.
