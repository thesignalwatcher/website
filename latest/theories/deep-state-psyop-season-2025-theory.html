Voice of Reason

The theory under examination holds that the year 2025 was deliberately "opened" by a "deep state" as a coordinated season of staged psychological operations, each event scripted to keep the public disoriented while government overreach advanced unnoticed, with the news media serving as an operational arm of the manipulation rather than as independent reporters. Every major incident is claimed to be a component of a predetermined playbook, and the simultaneity of events is itself presented as proof of coordination.

The factual record of early 2025 is rich and fully documented — and it dismantles every specific premise. The two most-cited anchors for this genre of claim were the Los Angeles wildfires of January 7 and the midair collision over the Potomac River on January 29. On January 7, wildfires erupted across the Los Angeles metro area, killing more than two dozen people, razing upward of fifteen thousand homes and businesses, and moving immediately into the second and third spots on California's all-time list of most destructive fires. A 32-researcher international team led by the World Weather Attribution project concluded that climate change increased the risk of the devastating LA wildfires. More specifically, preliminary research indicates that the warm and dry conditions fueling these fires were 35% more likely to occur due to climate change. The proximate environmental conditions were not mysterious: the fires were unprecedented in both spread and intensity largely due to extreme Santa Ana winds and critically low vegetation moisture early in the year, stemming from one of the warmest summers on record in 2024 and an exceptionally dry fall, with almost no rain during a season when rainfall is normally expected. As for the Potomac collision, the NTSB concluded the fatal collision between an American Airlines regional jet and Army Black Hawk helicopter was the result of a "multitude of errors" and "systemic issues across multiple organizations." The board found that the FAA's placement of Route 4 was a probable cause of the crash, pointing to a structural error embedded in the airspace design, and disclosed that since 2021 there were 15,200 air separation incidents near Reagan between commercial airplanes and helicopters, including 85 close-call events — a pre-existing systemic hazard that had nothing to do with any political moment in January 2025. These events were investigated by independent federal bodies, subjected to public congressional scrutiny, analyzed by scientists across multiple countries, and produced voluminous documentary records. None of this is consistent with a staged operation; staged events do not generate multi-agency investigations whose findings contradict the staging hypothesis.

The theory's deepest flaw is not factual but structural: it is designed to be unfalsifiable, and the "evidence" it presents is pure apophenia. In 2008, Michael Shermer coined the word "patternicity," defining it as "the tendency to find meaningful patterns in meaningless noise." Research published in Scientific Reports in 2025 found that conspiracy believers may exhibit an overactive pattern recognition system, potentially linked to apophenia — the tendency to perceive meaningful patterns in random data. Conspiracy theories, superstitions, and various forms of magical thinking all involve the perception of meaningful patterns in events that are more parsimoniously explained by chance, coincidence, or unrelated causes. The "psyop season" frame is a near-perfect example of this dynamic: any cluster of unrelated events in a calendar year can be retrospectively narrated as a season, because major newsworthy events are a constant of political and social life, not an anomaly. The theory also has an impossible evidentiary burden it never acknowledges: coordinating simultaneous wildfires, aviation disasters, political transitions, and international conflicts across independent agencies, military branches, weather systems, and news organizations would require thousands of knowing participants, all maintaining perfect secrecy, across institutions with documented adversarial relationships with one another. No mechanism for this coordination is specified, no communications are cited, and no defection or leak from the alleged operation is explained away. Discussions on the deep state are fragmented across disciplines and the concept has not formed into a standalone idea even in academic literature — precisely because the empirical evidence for a unified, coordinating secret government has never materialized despite decades of search.

There is, however, a legitimate and well-documented concern that the theory hijacks. The intricate apophenia of today's QAnon-laced cynicism toward the federal government as deep state finds its origins in legitimate public concern about Cold War CIA covert operations. Real institutional failures — like the pre-existing aviation safety gaps the NTSB documented, or the decades of deferred wildfire policy in California — are exactly the kind of governmental shortcomings that erode public trust and create the emotional soil in which conspiracy theories grow. Allegations of a deep state conspiracy reached mainstream recognition under the first presidency of Donald Trump, who falsely claimed the "deep state" was working against him and his administration's agenda. That political framing then became a template applied indiscriminately: any bad event, any institutional failure, any coincidental timing becomes "the playbook." The crucial difference is that legitimate criticism of institutions is falsifiable — it names actors, cites documents, specifies mechanisms, and survives scrutiny. The "psyop season" narrative does none of these things. It converts every piece of news into confirmation and dismisses every investigation as part of the cover. Once the framework is accepted, nothing can disprove it — and that epistemic closure is the theory's primary harm.

That harm is real even if diffuse. Unraveling the roots of the "deep state" conspiracy theory reveals something important about the cynicism now infecting U.S. politics; in the U.S., the idea of a deep state cabal of unelected officials secretively pulling the strings of American government is widely believed. When every major event is preemptively coded as manufactured, the public loses the capacity to evaluate institutional accountability on the merits: investigations are dismissed as theater, safety recommendations are ignored as misdirection, and the very processes that could identify and correct real failures — congressional hearings, independent journalism, scientific attribution studies — are treated as components of the deception. The theory does not produce vigilance. It produces paralysis, because it teaches its believers that independent analysis is futile by design.
