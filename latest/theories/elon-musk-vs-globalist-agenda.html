This cluster of claims holds that Elon Musk's decision to post a film on X — described as a "banned" European production suppressed by globalist institutions — proves he is waging a personal campaign against the World Economic Forum and the network of "unelected elites" secretly directing European demographic policy through deliberate depopulation and replacement. Every factual premise in this narrative can be checked against the public record, and the record dismantles each one. The film in question is *Citizen Vigilante*, a 2026 action thriller directed by Uwe Boll and starring Armie Hammer. It is a vigilante action thriller produced, written, and directed by Boll, starring Hammer as a vigilante who targets criminal migrants and corrupt officials, and it was commercially released in select theaters and digitally on June 19, 2026. It was not suppressed by any globalist institution. The film received a normal commercial release in the United States and Canada via Quiver Distribution. It is available on Amazon Prime Video and Apple TV. The distribution barrier in Germany is real but narrow and bureaucratic: the film has not been banned by government decree; the FSK, Germany's age-rating board, refused to grant it any classification, meaning without that authorization it cannot be shown in cinemas, sold in physical format, or distributed on mainstream platforms inside Germany — in practice, it has been blocked from the German market. According to Boll, the FSK denied the film an age certification for promoting vigilantism, though the film has not been placed on the formal "Index," making it legal to own or sell to adults. A film available for commercial purchase globally, streaming on major platforms, and reviewed by Variety, the Hollywood Reporter, and Rotten Tomatoes is not a suppressed document — it is a low-budget action movie that anyone with an Amazon account can watch today. Musk's post of the full film on X drew over 10.5 million views, an outcome Musk himself framed not as a revelation of hidden truths but as a textbook Streisand Effect: Musk made several posts praising the film and suggested that efforts to ban it had boosted its reach, specifically mentioning the "Streisand Effect." As for the film's content, reviewers from Variety to Rotten Tomatoes found it to be a low-quality exploitation thriller, not a documentary of coordinated elite agendas. Variety's review noted that the film "manages to be so indulgent and incurious a portrait of a man exacting vengeance that calling it wish-fulfillment feels irresponsible," describing it as "pointlessly nonlinear" with no real plot beyond a series of brutal vigilante acts. Nowhere in any review, summary, or primary-source account does the film document "depopulation plans," "demographic replacement agendas," or coordination by supranational elites — because it contains none of those things.
