Voice of Reason

The theory being addressed here holds that Newsmax host Rob Schmitt's claim is true: that Democrats intentionally worked to make the New York Knicks lose Game 3 of the 2026 NBA Finals so they could use the defeat to embarrass Donald Trump and his supporters, and that Democratic "hype" over Trump's attendance was the actual cause of the loss.

The established facts are straightforward and leave no room for this framing. The New York Knicks, coming off two straight wins in the Finals against the San Antonio Spurs, lost at home 115-111 on Monday night — Game 3 of the 2026 NBA Finals — with Trump watching from a luxury suite at Madison Square Garden. Trump became the first sitting president to attend the NBA Finals during that game. The on-court explanation for the loss is fully documented by sports journalists and analysts. The Spurs had blown double-digit leads in Games 1 and 2, but after falling behind by seven points at halftime of Game 3, they responded with a dominant 35-27 third quarter — easily their best offensive quarter of the series. Analytical trends that had favored the Knicks in the first two games reversed: Victor Wembanyama was stronger in the fourth period, the Knicks failed to convert corner threes, and San Antonio's ball control and low turnover rate provided enough for a narrow win. Knicks coach Mike Brown pointed specifically to the Spurs' 24-8 advantage in free throw attempts in the second half as a contributing factor. In short, two professional basketball teams played a competitive game and one of them won, for reasons that ESPN, NBA.com, and multiple sports analysts documented in granular detail.

The conspiracy theory, articulated on-air by Newsmax host Rob Schmitt, asserts that Democrats "wanted the Knicks to lose last night so that they could blame it on Donald Trump" and that their collective "energy" made it happen. This claim fails at every level of basic reasoning. First, it proposes no mechanism whatsoever — there is no described method by which Democratic politicians or commentators could alter the shooting percentages, free throw calls, or defensive schemes of NBA players. Second, the theory is unfalsifiable by design: if the Knicks had won, the theory would presumably vanish; because they lost, the result is retrofitted as proof. Third, it inverts the actual sequence of events: the watch parties, security commentary, and fan frustration directed at Trump's attendance were reactions to Trump's announced presence, not a coordinated pre-game campaign. The watch party outside Madison Square Garden was canceled after the U.S. Secret Service and the NYPD determined it could not be held due to security requirements associated with the president's attendance. The Secret Service and the NYPD jointly determined that outdoor watch parties could not be accommodated in the immediate vicinity of MSG due to security requirements associated with an event of this scale. The disruption that fans experienced — canceled gatherings, long lines, a security perimeter spanning several city blocks — was not manufactured by Democratic operatives but was the standard and legally mandated consequence of a presidential visit to a major urban venue. California Governor Gavin Newsom playfully engaged the "Trump jinx" framing on social media, and Rep. Jerry Nadler, at a congressional hearing on the Sports Broadcasting Act, quipped that as long as Trump stayed away from MSG, the Knicks would be fine. These were rhetorical jokes made after the game, not evidence of a pre-planned scheme — and neither Nadler's wisecrack nor Newsom's repost has any causal relationship to the Spurs' third-quarter surge.

There is a kernel of legitimate frustration embedded in this episode, and it deserves acknowledgment. Trump's attendance genuinely did cause friction for many New Yorkers: watch parties were canceled, a strict security perimeter was implemented around the arena, and fans were forced to wait in lengthy security lines. Fans were not allowed to bring bags into the arena, and selfie sticks, glass containers, and other common items were also prohibited under Secret Service rules. These are real inconveniences that real fans experienced, and criticism of that disruption is legitimate democratic commentary, not sabotage. The conspiracy theory, however, takes that legitimate grievance and launders it into something unrecognizable: a secret, coordinated plot by an entire political party to supernaturally influence the outcome of a professional basketball game. It is worth noting that Trump's attendance does not even guarantee the home team will lose — last September, for instance, the New York Yankees beat the visiting Detroit Tigers 9-3 while Trump was present. The pattern that media outlets described as a "Trump curse" is itself a statistical artifact drawn from a small, cherry-picked sample of games — exactly the kind of anecdote-as-data thinking that Schmitt's segment then weaponized into a partisan accusation.

The broader harm here is contextual but significant. Newsmax has already paid $67 million to settle a defamation lawsuit brought by Dominion Voting Systems for spreading false claims about the 2020 election, and separately paid approximately $40 million to settle a similar lawsuit from voting machine manufacturer Smartmatic. Lawsuits in both cases showed how some of the president's biggest supporters knew the claims being aired were false at the time. That institutional track record matters: a network with a demonstrated history of broadcasting provably false conspiratorial claims for political purposes is not a neutral narrator when it tells its audience that Democrats are engineering sports outcomes. The "Democrats sabotaged the Knicks" claim produces no specific allegation that could be investigated, no evidence that could be examined, and no prediction that could be falsified. It exists purely to convert a basketball loss into partisan grievance — and to train viewers to interpret ordinary events through a lens of malicious, hidden coordination, a habit of mind that corrodes the capacity for accurate judgment about everything else.
