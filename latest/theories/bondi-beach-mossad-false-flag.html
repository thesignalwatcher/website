Voice of Reason

There is no kernel of verified fact here to anchor anything — and that absence matters. When a claim about a specific event (a named location, a precise date, named perpetrators, leaked documents, a Royal Commission) arrives with zero verifiable sources, the right first question is not whether the explanation sounds plausible, but whether the event and evidence it describes can be confirmed at all.

The claim works by layering detail — dates, agency names, document leaks, institutional cover-ups — because specificity feels like proof. But named details that cannot be checked are not evidence; they are the appearance of evidence. Notice also the structure: every absence of proof (no documents surface, no commission confirms it) gets folded into the cover-up itself, which means nothing could ever count against the claim. When a theory is built so that missing evidence only deepens suspicion, it has stopped being a theory you can test. The plainer question worth sitting with: if a real attack occurred, what would honest investigation of the actual perpetrator look like — and does this claim point toward that, or away from it?
