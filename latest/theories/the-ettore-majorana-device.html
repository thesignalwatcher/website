Voice of Reason

Ettore Majorana was a real and genuinely remarkable physicist. His theoretical work in the 1930s was so advanced that Enrico Fermi reportedly said Majorana belonged in a class with Newton and Galileo. He did disappear in 1938 under circumstances that remain unresolved — he boarded a ferry from Palermo, was never seen again, and left behind letters suggesting he intended to vanish. That much is documented and genuinely mysterious.

The leap comes here: an unexplained disappearance gets read as proof of suppression, and suppression becomes proof of a world-altering device — none of which has any evidentiary basis. Majorana left no record of applied engineering work, no device, no collaborators describing one. The claim only holds together if you first accept that a vast, coordinated suppression machine exists and that absence of evidence is itself part of the cover. Once that assumption is in place, every gap in the record feels like confirmation. But a feeling of pattern is not the same as a pattern. The simpler read is that a brilliant, reportedly troubled man chose to disappear — which, on its own, is already a compelling historical mystery. What would it take to move from that toward the transmutation claim?
