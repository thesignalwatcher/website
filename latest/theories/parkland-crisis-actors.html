Voice of Reason

Seventeen people were killed at Marjory Stoneman Douglas High School on February 14, 2018. That is documented by law enforcement, medical examiners, and court records. Survivors spoke publicly and some, including David Hogg, became visible advocates for gun-law changes. Those are the facts the claims are built on.

The leap happens when ordinary details get re-read as planted evidence. A teen filmed video on the day of the shooting — that becomes proof he wasn't really there. His father once worked for the FBI — that becomes a covert script. An arm raised at a rally — that becomes a salute. Each detail is genuinely ambiguous, but once you assume a hidden operation is running, ambiguity stops feeling like ambiguity and starts feeling like confirmation. That feeling of pieces clicking into place is not the same as evidence.

The premise only holds if you accept a conspiracy large enough to include law enforcement, a major network, a political party, and every classmate who corroborated the survivors' accounts — and large enough that the absence of any proof of staging counts as further proof of how well it was hidden. At that point, the theory has protected itself from any evidence at all. The simpler read: traumatized teenagers became activists. Is that consistent with everything known about the day?
