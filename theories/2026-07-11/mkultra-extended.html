Voice of Reason

MKUltra was real. Declassified documents confirm the CIA ran coercive behavioral experiments on unwitting subjects for roughly two decades, and the 1977 Senate hearings confirmed that Director Richard Helms ordered files destroyed in 1973. That destruction is genuinely troubling — it is a real gap in the historical record, and acknowledging that does not require guessing what filled the gap.

Here is where the reasoning stretches: a gap in documents gets reread as proof of continuation. But absence of a full record is not the same as evidence of an ongoing program. The claim then links that gap to mass shootings, celebrity behavior, and modern surveillance — very different phenomena — by assuming they all share a hidden cause. Once you accept a secret machine large enough to program shooters, manage celebrities, and run surveillance undetected for fifty years, no missing evidence can count against it, because the machine's job is to hide the evidence. That is a closed loop, not a finding. The simpler read is that a real, documented abuse was ended under congressional pressure, its perpetrators faced limited accountability, and that genuine outrage — entirely warranted — is now being channeled toward claims the record does not support.
