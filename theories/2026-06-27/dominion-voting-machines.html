Voice of Reason

Dominion Voting Systems is a real company whose machines were used in several states in 2020, and some voters genuinely distrust electronic voting equipment — that unease is understandable. What the evidence actually shows is that post-election audits in Georgia, Arizona, Michigan, and elsewhere, including hand recounts, consistently matched the machine totals. More than sixty courts reviewed fraud claims and found none supported by evidence. Dominion sued Fox News and others for defamation over these allegations and reached a substantial settlement — a legal outcome that required those outlets to confront what their own internal communications showed they knew.

The leap the claim makes is treating unexplained-looking data patterns as proof of intent, then working backward to construct the machinery that must have caused them. Late vote-count shifts had a documented reason: many states processed mail ballots last, and those broke heavily Democratic. Once you assume a coordinated hidden operation, every ambiguous detail — a data spike, a delayed count, an uncorroborated affidavit — feels like it fits. But that feeling of fit is what happens when a framework is unfalsifiable, not when it is confirmed. Could every audit, every court, and every recount have been part of the same cover-up — or is there a simpler read?

Origins & History

Claims that Dominion Voting Systems machines secretly flipped votes from Donald Trump to Joe Biden emerged in mid-November 2020, with Trump tweets appearing between approximately November 12–17, 2020, and Rudy Giuliani making related assertions on Fox News around November 15. The claims reached a wider audience through a press conference held by Giuliani and Sidney Powell on November 19, 2020 — sometimes referred to as the "Kraken" press conference — at which Powell alleged that secret software had manipulated vote tallies.

The framing drew on earlier, related narratives, including claims involving a purported "Hammer and Scorecard" supercomputer program and longstanding allegations connecting voting technology companies to Venezuelan political figures, particularly Hugo Chávez.

Amplification occurred rapidly through Fox News, Newsmax, and One America News Network during November 2020, and the claims were incorporated into a series of legal filings informally called the "Kraken" lawsuits. The 2022 documentary *2000 Mules* represented a later iteration of related election-fraud claims, though with somewhat different evidentiary framing.

Dominion Voting Systems responded by filing defamation suits against multiple individuals and media organizations. Fox News reached a settlement with Dominion in April 2023 for approximately $787.5 million, without a trial verdict on the underlying factual claims. CBS News's *60 Minutes* reported on the Dominion conspiracy theories and the company's legal responses in October 2022 ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/dominion-voting-systems-conspiracy-theories-60-minutes-report-2022-10-23/)).
