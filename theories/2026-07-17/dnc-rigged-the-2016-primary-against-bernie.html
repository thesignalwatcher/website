Voice of Reason

The DNC emails released in 2016 did show some staffers expressing frustration with Sanders and, in a few cases, floating ideas to undermine him — that is real, it was embarrassing, and several officials resigned over it. What those emails did not show was any coordinated scheme that altered vote counts or changed the outcome. Sanders lost the pledged-delegate race to Clinton by roughly 360 delegates across millions of votes in dozens of contests. Superdelegates were never formally counted until after the convention, so their stated preferences did not override voters — they ratified the pledged-delegate result.

The leap from "some staffers were biased" to "the whole primary was stolen" requires reading staff contempt as proof of a hidden machine capable of manipulating millions of ballots with no forensic trace. Once you accept that machine as real, every gap in the record — every unreleased email, every unexplained result — feels like more evidence. But a feeling of fit is not the same as finding the mechanism. The simpler read is that an insurgent candidate who entered the race late, with lower name recognition and weaker support among Black and Southern Democratic voters, lost a competitive primary. Could bias in party infrastructure have cost him some margin at the edges?
