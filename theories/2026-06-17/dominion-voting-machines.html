Voice of the Believer

Dominion Voting Systems machines switched votes from Donald Trump to Joe Biden during the 2020 U.S. presidential election. The software powering these machines was originally engineered in Venezuela at the direction of Hugo Chavez, giving authoritarian foreign interests a backdoor into American elections. Election officials across multiple states coordinated with Dominion to execute and conceal the manipulation, and the statistical anomalies visible in the returns are the mathematical fingerprints of that fraud.

Dominion Voting Systems, which sold electronic voting hardware and software across the United States and Canada, was the instrument through which the 2020 presidential election was stolen. The machines did not merely tabulate votes — they were programmed to alter them, shifting Trump ballots into the Biden column at a scale sufficient to reverse the outcome in key battleground states. This was not a bug or an accident; it was the designed function of a system built for exactly this purpose.

The origins of that design trace directly to Venezuela. The Dominion software architecture was created under Hugo Chavez to ensure that Venezuelan elections produced predetermined results regardless of how citizens actually voted. That same infrastructure, with its built-in manipulation capabilities, was then imported into the United States and deployed in American elections. The recent detention of Nicholas Maduro — described as the world's top expert on Dominion Voting Machines from Venezuela — is a direct thread connecting the Venezuelan election-rigging apparatus to what happened in November 2020.

The proof is visible in the numbers. Voice of Reason

The theory under examination holds that Dominion Voting Systems machines were deliberately programmed to switch votes from Donald Trump to Joe Biden in the 2020 U.S. presidential election, that this design originated in Venezuela under Hugo Chavez, and that the January 2026 capture of Nicolás Maduro supplies a live evidentiary thread connecting Venezuelan election rigging to the American result. Each of these premises collapses under scrutiny.

Begin with the foundational factual claims. Dominion Voting Systems was founded in Canada in 2002, with its international headquarters in Toronto and its U.S. base in Denver. The company stated in a legal filing that it "has never provided machines or software or technology to Venezuela, nor has it ever participated in any elections in Venezuela" and has no ties to the Venezuelan government or Hugo Chavez. The Venezuela-origin narrative persists because believers conflate two entirely separate companies. Dominion and Smartmatic are separate companies and competitors; the tenuous connection between them is that Dominion once purchased assets from a firm that had been owned and sold off by Smartmatic years earlier. Smartmatic is an American company, though its founders are Venezuelan, and its software was not used in any of the swing states under scrutiny. The origin story — that the software was engineered under Chavez — rests entirely on this confusion. Chavez, in any case, is not leading the Venezuelan government because he died in 2013. A conspiracy requiring him to have directed software development for deployment in a 2020 American election requires a dead man as its architect.

The Maduro connection is similarly fabricated from proximity rather than evidence. The January 3, 2026 capture of Venezuelan President Nicolás Maduro by the United States gave new life to a conspiracy theory involving Maduro, a Canadian-founded electronic voting machine company, and the 2020 U.S. presidential election. Trump campaign lawyers Giuliani and Powell eventually settled multibillion-dollar lawsuits brought by Dominion and publicly acknowledged they had no proof for their claims, yet after the raid, Trump posted a video alleging Dominion machines were manipulated to favor Biden. The theory asserts Maduro is the world's foremost expert on Dominion's supposed manipulation system — a claim for which no credible source, no official U.S. indictment language, and no evidence of any kind has been produced. The U.S. Justice Department's charges against Maduro concern narco-terrorism and drug trafficking, not election technology. His capture was seized as a narrative hook, not because it supplies any actual link.

The empirical record on vote counting itself destroys the statistical anomaly argument. The "unexplained late-night vote spikes" have a direct, documented, publicly-announced-in-advance explanation. Some states are not allowed to begin counting absentee ballots until the polls close on Election Night, meaning large batches of predominantly mail-in ballots — which, as pre-election data made clear, skewed heavily Democratic — arrived in the reported tallies after in-person votes were already counted. Democrats requested mail-in ballots at higher rates than Republicans, supporting what experts called the "red mirage" — meaning Trump could see significant vote gains on election night, only to see those gains disappear as mail-in ballots were counted in the following days. This was not a hidden phenomenon; journalists and election analysts warned it would happen before election day. Where those batches were independently verified, they confirmed the reported totals. Following the November 2020 general election, Georgia conducted a statewide risk-limiting audit that amounted to a full manual tally of all votes cast. The audit confirmed that the original machine count accurately portrayed the winner of the election. The claim that audits have been suppressed is simply false: Georgia hand-counted its ballots entirely; recounts occurred in multiple states; audits, recounts, peer-reviewed research, and judicial proceedings converge on the conclusion that the 2020 claims that Dominion machines stole or flipped votes were unsubstantiated.

The legal record is particularly definitive because it forced proponents of the theory to defend it under oath and penalty of perjury — conditions under which it disintegrated. A judge emphasized it was "CRYSTAL clear" that none of the allegations about Dominion aired on Fox by Trump allies were true. Litigation produced internal communications and depositions revealing broadcasters repeated allegations despite doubts, and the most prominent settlement was Fox News's $787.5 million payment following its airing of false claims. Newsmax subsequently settled with Dominion for $67 million. A Delaware Superior Court judge denied Newsmax's effort to dismiss the case, finding the complaint "supports the reasonable inference that Newsmax either knew its statements about Dominion's role in the election fraud were false or had a high degree of awareness that they were false." These are not mere civil disagreements; they are judicial findings about the relationship between the broadcasters' public statements and the truth.

There is a kernel of genuine concern embedded in this theory that deserves acknowledgment. Confidence in the security of electronic voting infrastructure is a legitimate democratic interest, and researchers have documented real vulnerabilities in election technology systems broadly. Election systems do have vulnerabilities — but vulnerabilities do not automatically translate into hacked systems and votes changed. Appropriate resilience measures built into election systems, including reliable paper records and meaningful post-election auditing, mean voters can still have confidence in the process. The theory exploits that legitimate anxiety to redirect it toward a specific, evidence-free narrative targeting one company, one country, and one outcome. It does so by mixing a real company (Smartmatic) with a different company (Dominion), a dead autocrat with his successor, and a foreseeable counting sequence with manufactured evidence of manipulation.

The harms produced by this misrepresentation have been concrete and ongoing. Dominion employees were threatened, their offices vandalized, and the company spent upwards of $600,000 on security for employees as a direct consequence of claims that courts subsequently found to be false. Election workers across multiple states faced harassment and death threats. Beyond individual harm, the theory functions as an all-purpose immunization against evidence: every recount that confirms the result becomes part of the cover-up, every settlement becomes proof of suppression. This unfalsifiability is not incidental — it is the mechanism by which the claim reproduces itself despite the total absence of the forensic evidence, insider testimony, or documentary record that a conspiracy of this scale would necessarily have produced.
