Voice of the Believer

MKUltra never stopped — the CIA's mind control program continued underground after its nominal closure and operates today. Mass shooters are programmed assets activated on command, celebrities function as mind-controlled slaves managed by their handlers, and the surveillance infrastructure that now blankets modern society grew directly out of MKUltra's research into behavioral control.

MKUltra did not end with the 1973 document destruction or the 1977 Senate hearings. Those events were a controlled retreat designed to move the program out of sight. The CIA transferred its behavioral research into classified successor projects, where the work of breaking and rebuilding human minds continued without congressional oversight or public scrutiny. The original program always extended far beyond the publicly acknowledged experiments — Subproject 11 and others like it pushed into psychological manipulation techniques that went well beyond anything admitted in declassified files, reaching into the operational use of conditioned human assets.

Mass shootings are not the spontaneous acts of disturbed individuals. The shooters are subjects who were identified, groomed, and programmed over years — then activated through triggers when a political or social outcome is required. The timing of these events, and the shooter profiles that follow predictable patterns, are the signature of a managed program, not random violence. The official investigations never look for the handlers because the agencies running the investigations are the same agencies running the program.

Their public personas, breakdowns, and behavioral cycles are the visible surface of an ongoing control system. Handlers manage their access, their messaging, and their reach into mass culture, making them vectors for the broader behavioral influence operation.

The mass surveillance architecture built after 9/11 — data collection, behavioral profiling, social media monitoring — is the technological maturation of MKUltra's core project: mapping and controlling human behavior at scale. What was once done to individuals in black sites is now done to entire populations through infrastructure. The continuity is direct and intentional.

Voice of Reason

The claim at the center of this theory is that the CIA's MKUltra program never ended, that it operates today as a classified successor directing mass shooters, controlling celebrities, and evolving into modern mass surveillance — in short, that every major strand of American behavioral pathology in public life traces back to a single, continuously managed government program.

The foundational historical premise deserves engagement first, because MKUltra was real, its abuses were genuine, and the documentary record supports serious criticism of the CIA. MKUltra was a top-secret CIA project in which the agency conducted hundreds of clandestine experiments — sometimes on unwitting U.S. citizens — to assess the potential use of LSD and other drugs for mind control, information gathering, and psychological torture; details of the illicit program did not become public until 1975, during a congressional investigation into widespread illegal CIA activities. MKUltra itself was technically closed out in 1964, but some of its work was transferred to the Office of Research and Development under the name MKSEARCH and continued into the 1970s. CIA director Richard Helms and longtime MKUltra chief Sidney Gottlieb destroyed most of the original project records in 1973. About 8,000 pages of records — mostly financial documents mistakenly not destroyed — were found in 1977, launching a second round of inquiries. The 1977 Senate joint hearings, convened by Senators Inouye and Kennedy, produced extensive sworn testimony and publicly documented a closely held CIA project conducted from 1953 to 1964, described as an "umbrella project" under which sensitive subprojects were funded, involving among other things research on drugs and behavioral modification. The recovered material identified 185 non-government researchers and assistants involved in 149 subprojects. Subproject 11, frequently invoked in conspiracy tellings as evidence of sinister behavioral programming, is actually documented in the surviving records as a toxicology project: it involved developing and stockpiling toxins — specifically, identifying and producing in sufficient quantity the active ingredients in certain seeds and plants, and preparing a supply of the toxic protein Abrin. The claim that Subproject 11 or comparable subprojects developed a reliable operational system for creating "conditioned human assets" who could be triggered remotely is not supported by any surviving document, by any Senate testimony, or by any peer-reviewed account of the program. What the record actually shows is that researchers repeatedly failed to achieve reliable behavioral control: LSD produced unpredictable and chaotic results, hypnosis experiments yielded no consistent operational capability, and the program's own internal assessments described key lines of research as "probably not a high pay-off program."

The specific claims about continuation, mass shooters, and celebrities require the theory's proponents to supply a mechanism they never provide. The "controlled retreat" hypothesis — that the 1973 document destruction and the 1977 hearings were a cover story — is unfalsifiable by design: every piece of counter-evidence (the openness of the hearings, the creation of new congressional intelligence oversight committees, the ongoing FOIA releases now numbering over 1,200 pages) is reinterpreted as part of the cover. The committee's findings led to important reforms, including President Ford's executive order banning political assassinations and the creation of the Senate Select Committee on Intelligence in 1976 as a permanent oversight body. That is an institutional footprint of termination and restraint, not of continuation. The theory also requires believing that no participant among the hundreds of researchers, contractors, university employees, prison officials, and foreign intelligence partners involved over decades has defected, documented, or disclosed the current program — an implausible scale of secret-keeping with no historical analogue. As for mass shooters being "programmed assets," published clinical research directly contradicts this. The majority of assailants studied — 87.5% — had misdiagnosed and incorrectly treated, or undiagnosed and untreated, psychiatric illness; most also experienced profound estrangement from families, friends, and classmates, which rendered them more vulnerable to their untreated illness and to radicalization online, which fostered their violence. Psychosis or disorganization related to mental illness were infrequently the primary motivational factor in mass murders (9.6% of cases studied), while the vast majority of incidents — nearly 58% — were impulsive and emotionally driven, following adverse life circumstances. The "predictable profiles" believers cite as evidence of a managed program are in fact the predictable profiles of untreated mental illness, social isolation, and radicalization — phenomena thoroughly documented in psychiatric and criminological literature without any reference to CIA handlers.

The theory does tap into genuine concerns worth acknowledging. MKUltra was a real, documented program in which the U.S. government subjected citizens to harm without consent, destroyed evidence of that harm, and for years stonewalled congressional investigators. The post-9/11 expansion of domestic surveillance infrastructure — through the PATRIOT Act, NSA bulk collection programs, and commercial data brokering — represents a legitimate area of civil liberties concern that independent journalists, courts, and oversight bodies have scrutinized for years. These are real grievances, and they deserve the serious, evidence-based inquiry they have in fact received. The conspiracy theory, however, converts those legitimate concerns into an unfalsifiable, all-encompassing framework in which every celebrity breakdown is handler-managed, every mass shooting is a triggered asset, and any absence of evidence for the ongoing program is proof of how well it is concealed. That move — from documented historical wrong to omnipotent current operation explaining everything — is precisely the reasoning failure that makes the theory analytically worthless and practically dangerous.

The harm it produces is concrete. Sandy Hook conspiracy theorists accused family members of those killed, survivors, religious leaders, neighbors, and first responders of being part of a government plot, and Newtown parents were accused of faking their children's deaths or their very existence. The families testified in court that the lies spread by conspiracy theorists led to harassment and threats from people who accused them of faking their own children's deaths. One woman, sentenced to five months in prison followed by five months of home detention, had pleaded guilty to interstate transmission of threats to injure in communications with the father of a child killed at Sandy Hook. Alex Jones was found liable by default in defamation cases filed by Sandy Hook families. Beyond individual victims, the framework causes structural harm by distracting from the many risk factors that research shows are important to address before they lead to violence — and by attributing mental health crises to government programming, it actively discourages the psychiatric treatment and community intervention that evidence identifies as the most reliable paths to prevention.
