Explanation of Belief

Claims that a video exists showing Hillary Clinton and Huma Abedin assaulting and mutilating a child, supposedly recovered from Anthony Weiner's laptop in a file nicknamed 'Life insurance' and circulated within QAnon.

Origins: Surfaced in 2018 on 4chan/8chan and YouTube as an outgrowth of Pizzagate. No such video has ever been produced, recovered, or verified by any law-enforcement agency or news organization.

How believers defend it: Adherents insist the video was genuine but scrubbed by authorities and platforms — 'I saw the Frazzledrip video before they removed it' — and treat its absence as proof of a cover-up rather than of nonexistence. Censorship and takedowns are read as confirmation.

Voice of Reason

There is no confirmed kernel of fact here — no verified video, no corroborating police report, no named officer whose death has been linked to this claim by any investigation. That absence matters, because the claim has been circulating for years with a consistent promise: the footage is always just about to be released. It never arrives, and that gap keeps getting explained away as further proof of suppression.

The move that carries the whole weight is this: take a real event (NYPD investigators did examine Anthony Weiner's laptop during a separate criminal case) and read every ambiguity inside that case as deliberate concealment by powerful people who mean harm. Once that assumption is in place, the lack of evidence feels like evidence — because of course they would hide it. That feeling of pieces clicking together is what the claim is designed to produce, not what it has demonstrated. Any claim structured so that missing proof confirms the theory rather than weakens it has freed itself from the normal way we check whether something is true. The simpler question worth sitting with: if this footage genuinely existed and dozens of people had seen it, what would need to be true for none of them to have preserved a copy by now?
