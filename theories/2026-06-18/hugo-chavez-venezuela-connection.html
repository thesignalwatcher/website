Hugo Chavez commissioned the development of voting software specifically engineered to manipulate election outcomes, and that technology was subsequently imported into the United States. Venezuelan operatives worked alongside domestic actors to deploy this system during the 2020 presidential election, ensuring a fraudulent result.

Hugo Chavez directed the creation of vote-rigging software designed from the ground up to allow election administrators to alter tallies and override the will of voters. The software was built to be undetectable under normal auditing procedures, giving operators the ability to shift vote totals at will while producing paperwork that appeared legitimate. After Chavez's death, the infrastructure and the operatives who maintained it did not disappear — they were preserved and eventually made available to foreign and domestic actors seeking to influence elections elsewhere.

The technology was imported into the United States and integrated into voting systems used in the 2020 presidential election. Venezuelan operatives with direct ties to the Chavez-era election manipulation apparatus played an active role in this operation, providing technical expertise and coordination. The result was a stolen election in which Donald Trump's genuine victory was erased and Joe Biden was installed as president through manufactured vote totals.

The 2020 election outcome is not a reflection of American voters' choices but of a foreign-engineered system operating inside American election infrastructure. The official narrative of a free and fair election is a cover for what amounts to a foreign interference operation with roots in the Bolivarian socialist project Chavez built in Venezuela. The same mechanisms used to keep Chavez and his allies in power were repurposed to determine the outcome of an American presidential race.

Voice of Reason

This theory holds that Hugo Chávez personally commissioned vote-rigging software, that this technology survived his death and was subsequently transplanted into American election infrastructure, and that it was used by Venezuelan operatives to manufacture Joe Biden's victory in the 2020 presidential election. Every factual premise of that chain is either demonstrably false or unsupported by any verifiable evidence.

Start with the foundational corporate claim. Prominent Trump figures including Rudy Giuliani and attorney Sidney Powell falsely claimed that voting machine vendors Dominion and Smartmatic were secretly Venezuelan companies founded to rig elections for Hugo Chávez. Both facts are wrong. Both companies' origins are public information and well documented: Dominion Voting Systems was founded in 2002 in Toronto, Canada, and was purchased by a former GOP election official in 2025. Smartmatic was incorporated in Delaware in 2000 by three Venezuelan-born engineers who had long since left their home country. Dominion CEO John Poulos was unambiguous when asked directly whether his company had any connection to Chávez or used Venezuelan software: "Absolutely not," he said, adding: "We were founded in Toronto — which is where my family was from. And there's nothing to do with Venezuela." Dominion has no ties to China, Venezuela, George Soros, or Smartmatic. The two companies are not related entities — they are competitors. The only indirect connection is that Dominion in 2010 purchased assets from Sequoia Voting Systems, and Smartmatic briefly owned Sequoia between 2005 and 2007. Powell's claim appears to be little more than an effort to insinuate guilt by very indirect association with an authoritarian regime.

Smartmatic's history is the legitimate kernel of fact from which this theory is constructed — and it is telling how far the theory strays from it. Smartmatic's founders are Venezuelan, and the company secured a $91 million contract in May 2004 to provide voting machines for national elections under President Hugo Chávez, amid allegations that its rapid rise benefited from regime favoritism. That is a real and appropriately scrutinized episode in the company's past. But the leap from "Smartmatic ran elections in Venezuela" to "Smartmatic and Dominion stole the 2020 U.S. election for Biden" requires ignoring a decisive fact: Smartmatic was operating in just one jurisdiction in the U.S. during the 2020 elections — Los Angeles County, which was all but guaranteed to swing toward Biden in a heavily Democratic state. Smartmatic technology was used only in Los Angeles County, California in the 2020 election, and the system provided to LA County does not count, tabulate, or store votes. No swing state. No battleground county. The company the theory identifies as the mechanism of theft was irrelevant to every contested result. Even the specific claim that Smartmatic was "founded in Venezuela to fix elections" is factually wrong: Smartmatic was actually founded in 2000 in Boca Raton, Florida.

The theory also fails completely against the audit record. Following the November 2020 general election, Georgia's Secretary of State selected the presidential contest for a statewide risk-limiting audit — a full manual tally of all votes cast — and that audit confirmed that the original machine count accurately portrayed the winner of the election. A peer-reviewed study published in the Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, which compiled every available audit of votes cast in the 2020 United States elections at the county level or higher, found that the net error rate in counting presidential votes was on the order of thousandths of a percent. Hand counts matched machine counts across jurisdictions using different vendors and different systems. A secret algorithm altering tallies while producing legitimate-looking paperwork would have to have left no detectable trace across hundreds of independent audits conducted by officials of both parties — an operational impossibility that the theory never attempts to explain. There was no widespread fraud in the election, a fact confirmed by a range of election officials across the country including Trump's own attorney general, William Barr, as well as Republican governors in Arizona and Georgia.

The litigation record is equally decisive. Fox News settled Dominion's defamation lawsuit against it for $787 million in April 2023. Sidney Powell pleaded guilty in 2023 in a Georgia case in which the state accused her and others of attempting to overturn the 2020 results. Newsmax acknowledged that Smartmatic did not provide any technology or services outside of LA County or count votes in the 2020 election. These are not the outcomes of a suppressed truth — they are the outcomes of claims that could not survive legal scrutiny. The proponents of the Venezuela theory were not silenced; they were given every opportunity in depositions, discovery, and courtrooms to produce the evidence they claimed to possess. They produced none.

The legitimate concern embedded in this narrative — that electronic voting systems deserve rigorous, transparent oversight, and that foreign-connected vendors require careful vetting — is entirely valid as a policy matter. Those concerns have animated serious election-security scholarship for years. But this theory does not advance that debate; it hijacks it. It takes the real and verifiable history of Smartmatic's Venezuelan contracts, strips it of context, splices it together with a corporation (Dominion) that has no Venezuelan connection whatsoever, and asserts a coordinated foreign operation for which no operative, no code sample, no network log, no financial transaction, and no whistleblower has ever been produced. The result has been severe and documented harm: Dominion's CEO told CBS News's 60 Minutes that "people have been put into danger, all because of lies." Employees of both companies received death threats. The theory also corroded public confidence in the certification processes, hand-count audits, and bipartisan official findings that directly refute its claims — making citizens less able to trust the very verification mechanisms that demonstrate the theory is wrong.
