Voice of Reason

The claim circulating in the 2026 Iowa U.S. Senate race asserts that Democratic candidate Josh Turek supports "sex changes for kids" — implying he favors surgical or medical gender transition procedures for minors. PolitiFact rated this claim False. Here is what the verified record actually shows.

An ad from Republican candidate Ashley Hinson made this charge against Turek immediately after the June 2 primary, and Hinson's social media amplified the same phrase. The evidentiary basis the ad offered for this sweeping claim was a single legislative vote. The ad cited Turek's vote against a 2023 education law that required schools to notify parents if a student requested to use a different name or pronouns. That bill is Iowa Senate File 496. SF 496 changes the standards governing library and curricular materials that may be used in Iowa schools and requires parental notification of a student's request for accommodations related to gender identity. The law cited in the ad as evidence does not mention medical interventions or "sex changes" — it has to do with notifying parents when a student expresses a different gender identity at school. Turek's vote against SF 496 was a vote about school notification and library policy, not about surgery or medical treatment of any kind. Furthermore, a separate bill the same year banned gender-affirming medical treatments for minors; Turek was absent from that vote, and his campaign said he opposes such surgeries for minors.

The rhetorical mechanism at work here is a deliberate category collapse. The phrase "sex change" is not a standard medical term. Gender-affirming care can include a range of approaches to support a person's gender identity including, for minors, using a different name or pronouns; according to medical best practices, gender-affirming treatments are available only to adolescents and can include puberty blockers, hormone therapy, and in rare cases, surgeries for older teens. The ad fuses the broadest, most emotionally alarming end of that spectrum — surgical intervention — with the narrowest policy act it could pin on Turek: opposing a school-records notification rule. Although the ad showed video of surgeons operating, a Hinson campaign spokesperson said the ad was not referencing gender-affirming surgeries — an admission that the campaign itself acknowledged the surgical imagery did not correspond to what Turek actually voted on, while knowingly leaving viewers to make the connection anyway. This is a textbook technique of persuasion through implicature: the images do the work the text cannot defensibly assert.

There is a kernel of genuine policy disagreement embedded in this episode, and it deserves honest acknowledgment. Parental notification requirements for school-age children's gender-related requests are a real and contested area of education policy. Reasonable people disagree about the appropriate balance between a minor's privacy, a parent's right to know, and a school's duty of care. Turek is a self-described moderate Democrat who won his state house seat in a district that strongly supported Donald Trump, and during his tenure he has focused on disability policy, health care, and agricultural issues — a profile that does not fit the caricature of a radical ideologue seeking to impose medical procedures on children. The legitimate debate about where to draw lines in school policy was hijacked into an unfalsifiable smear: once a voter absorbs "sex changes for kids," no subsequent correction reaches the same emotional register as the original charge. That is precisely the design. The claim cannot be cleanly rebutted by pointing to voting records because it was never really about voting records — it was about fixing a frightening image to a name.

The concrete harm here operates on multiple levels. For Turek specifically, a false and inflammatory characterization injected into a high-stakes Senate race — a contest in which Republicans have won comfortably in Iowa for over a decade and in which the Republican-aligned Senate Leadership Fund plans to spend $29 million — can define a candidate's image with persuadable voters before any correction circulates. More broadly, the "sex changes for kids" framing functions as a wedge-issue template that has been applied to candidates across multiple cycles, regardless of their actual positions, because it exploits a genuine parental protective instinct and converts it into reflexive political opposition. When that framing is repeated uncritically across social media and low-scrutiny outlets, it crowds out factual discussion of what these laws actually do, who they affect, and what tradeoffs they involve — which is the precise kind of informed democratic deliberation it is designed to prevent.
