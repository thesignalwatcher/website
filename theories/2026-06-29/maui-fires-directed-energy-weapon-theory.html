The Maui wildfires of August 2023 were genuinely catastrophic, and the grief and anger surrounding them are completely understandable. What we do know from fire science is that the conditions that day — drought, dry grass from an invasive species, and hurricane-force winds — combined to create a fast-moving, hard-to-stop fire. The leap the claim makes is treating the scale of the destruction as proof of deliberate attack. Big, fast, deadly fires do happen under those exact weather conditions — no hidden technology required. Once you accept that powerful actors must be responsible, every ambiguous detail — unusual burn patterns, recovery priorities, policy decisions — feels like confirmation. That is the feeling of a pattern, not a demonstration of one. The claim only holds together if you first accept a coordinated operation so airtight that no missing physical evidence can count against it, which means virtually any future event could be folded into the same story. The simpler question worth sitting with: do the known weather and infrastructure failures fully account for what happened?
