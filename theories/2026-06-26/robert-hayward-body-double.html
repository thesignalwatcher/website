Voice of Reason

When video or images of a person look slightly "off" — skin texture, neckline, lighting — it's worth asking why before assuming the strangest explanation. Broadcast television compresses video, applies chroma correction, and streams through multiple encoding passes, all of which can flatten skin tones, sharpen edges in odd ways, and create banding artifacts right at a collar line. Camera angle, studio lighting raking across the face, and even the subject's own makeup for broadcast are all common sources of the visual oddities being pointed to here.

The leap happens at the moment those compression artifacts get reread as seam lines, and ordinary studio lighting gets reread as a disguise.
