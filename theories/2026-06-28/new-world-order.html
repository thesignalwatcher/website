Voice of Reason

Global institutions like the World Economic Forum and the United Nations do exist, do coordinate policy across borders, and are disproportionately shaped by wealthy and well-connected people. Those are real observations worth scrutiny. The better-supported account is that these institutions reflect the ordinary pull of money and influence over multilateral spaces — producing outcomes that favor powerful interests without requiring a unified secret command.

The leap here is treating coordination as conspiracy: because elites attend the same forums and sometimes share goals, they must be executing a single hidden plan. Once that assumption is in place, every ambiguous detail — a poorly explained policy, a revolving-door appointment, a redacted document — reads as confirmation. That feeling of pieces falling into place is a product of the framing, not independent evidence. The framework also requires a secret apparatus so vast and leak-proof that its absence from the public record becomes proof of its power. At that point, any unsupported claim travels free on one untested premise. The simpler read: powerful people coordinate, often badly, in ways that serve their own interests. Does that produce harmful outcomes? Possibly. Does it require a hidden master plan to explain? That is the question worth sitting with.
