Voice of Reason

The September 11 attacks were genuinely catastrophic, and it is reasonable to want exhaustive answers about how they were allowed to happen. Intelligence agencies did miss warnings, interagency communication failed, and post-attack policy choices — wars, surveillance expansion — were sweeping and controversial. Those are real, documented failures worth scrutiny.

The leap comes when each unanswered question gets reread as deliberate concealment. Collapse mechanics that structural engineers attribute to fire-weakened steel and progressive load transfer become proof of planted explosives. A damaged Pentagon facade that eyewitnesses and physical evidence tied to a large aircraft becomes proof of a missile. The pattern feels airtight because the premise — a hidden machine powerful enough to fake everything — absorbs any missing evidence as further proof of the cover-up. At that point, no gap in the record can count against the theory; it only confirms it. The emotionally charged extras (confiscated footage, impossible physics, government motive) sharpen the fear that powerful people intended harm — a fear that makes ambiguous details feel like they fit together. But the feeling of a pattern is not the same as evidence of one. The simpler read is that catastrophic failures, not coordinated malice, explain what happened. Whether the full story of those failures has ever been told is still worth asking.
