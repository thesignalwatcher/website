Origins & History

The claim now known as "Pizzagate" emerged around October 30, 2016, originating in discussions on 4chan's /pol/ board and Reddit communities, notably r/The_Donald. Its immediate catalyst was the release of hacked emails belonging to Hillary Clinton's campaign chairman John Podesta, published by WikiLeaks beginning October 7, 2016. Participants in these online communities interpreted certain words in the emails — most notably "cheese pizza" — as coded references to child sexual abuse, constructing an allegation that prominent Democratic figures were operating a child-sex-trafficking ring out of Comet Ping Pong, a Washington, D.C. pizzeria.

The claim spread rapidly from those forums to Twitter and YouTube in November 2016, and received significant amplification from media personalities including Alex Jones of Infowars. Scholars have noted the theory's ancestry in older narrative traditions, including 1980s satanic panic, blood libel, and longstanding "elite pedophile" lore.

Mainstream news coverage increased sharply in late November 2016, and the theory reached a broader public following a December 4, 2016 incident in which Edgar Maddison Welch entered Comet Ping Pong armed with a rifle, reportedly to "self-investigate" the claims. Google Trends data shows a pronounced spike in searches for the term during November–December 2016.

Prior documented coverage and debunkings are noted in sources including Britannica ([https://www.britannica.com/topic/Pizzagate](https://www.britannica.com/topic/Pizzagate)) and academic analysis of online conspiracy spread ([https://theconversation.com/how-conspiracy-theories-spread-online-its-not-just-down-to-algorithms-133891](https://theconversation.com/how-conspiracy-theories-spread-online-its-not-just-down-to-algorithms-133891)).
