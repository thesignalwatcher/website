Voice of the Believer

The United States government is under the control of Jewish and Zionist interests that direct American foreign policy toward Israel's benefit and away from the interests of white Americans. Jewish power extends beyond government into media and banking, where it operates as a coordinated system of influence that shapes public perception and economic outcomes. AIPAC functions as a vehicle for this control, funneling Israeli-aligned influence into American politics while avoiding registration as a foreign agent. The traditional framing of ZOG has evolved: some adherents now describe the structure not as a state apparatus but as a corporate-financial network — ZioCorp — that operates above and through nation-states.

The US government does not represent the American people — it represents Zionist interests. Jewish power, concentrated in finance, media, and political lobbying, directs the machinery of the state. AIPAC — the American-Israeli Political Action Committee — serves as the most visible instrument of this control, routing money and influence through Congress to ensure American foreign policy remains subordinated to Israel's strategic agenda. The demand that AIPAC register as a foreign agent, rather than operate as a domestic lobbying organization, reflects how thoroughly this arrangement has been normalized and protected.

Beyond the political system, Jewish control of media and polling ensures that public opinion itself is manufactured. Polls are not neutral instruments — they are controlled to produce results that serve Jewish interests, shaping what Americans believe is consensus and what they believe is fringe. This manufactured reality insulates the power structure from democratic accountability, since the very tools used to measure public sentiment are compromised at the source.

The architecture of this control has shifted over time. The older ZOG framing assumed a recognizable nation-state that had been captured. The more current understanding is that the structure has moved beyond any single government — it now operates as a transnational financial and corporate network, sometimes called ZioCorp, that uses nation-states as instruments rather than being contained within them. This makes the control harder to name and harder to resist, but more total in its reach.

White Americans are the primary domestic victims of this arrangement. Policy choices, cultural production, and economic outcomes are systematically oriented away from their interests. The government that is supposed to represent them has been repurposed to serve a different population's agenda — one rooted in ethnic loyalty and foreign state interests rather than in the welfare of the American people.

Voice of Reason

The ZOG (Zionist Occupied Government) conspiracy theory asserts that the United States government, its media, and its financial system are secretly directed by a coordinated Jewish power structure — not by American democratic processes — and that this arrangement systematically harms white Americans while serving Israeli and Jewish interests. The variant name "ZioCorp" updates the framing to a transnational corporate network, but the foundational claim is identical: Jewish ethnic coordination covertly controls the machinery of American life.

The theory's origins alone expose its character. The term "Zionist Occupation Government" and the acronym "ZOG" were coined in a 1976 article by the neo-Nazi activist Eric Thomson in *Liberty Bell* magazine. The concept was further developed and spread in the 1970s by American white supremacists, particularly Christian Identity adherents. It is used several times in *The Turner Diaries* (1978), a book written by white supremacist William Luther Pierce under the pseudonym Andrew MacDonald. ZOG is fully in line with the history of anti-Jewish representations and is directly linked to the idea of a Jewish plan for world domination as set out in the false document known as the *Protocols of the Elders of Zion*. The theory is not an independent empirical observation that arrived at a disturbing conclusion — it was engineered as an ideological weapon inside neo-Nazi organizing culture from the start, retrofitting centuries-old antisemitic templates onto contemporary politics.

Every specific empirical pillar of ZOG fails under scrutiny. The media control claim relies on viral figures — such as the assertion that "six Jewish companies own 96% of the world's media" — that are unsupported by credible evidence and lack verifiable methodology. The claim conflates representation in leadership with concentrated ownership; available analyses show some Jewish overrepresentation in executive roles, but not numerical ownership dominance or monopoly across U.S. media, with corporate complexity, diffuse public ownership, and differing definitions producing estimates of notable but limited percentages in senior roles. Major media companies are publicly traded corporations with diversified shareholder bases, governed by boards and fiduciary law, not by ethnic solidarity — a fact the theory never attempts to reconcile. The claim that polls are manufactured by Jewish-controlled organizations to simulate false consensus is a pure assertion with no evidentiary support at any level: no mechanism, no named organization, no leaked methodology, no whistleblower. It cannot be tested because its adherents treat any contradicting data point as itself evidence of the manipulation.

The AIPAC question deserves the most careful treatment because there are legitimate and ongoing policy debates about it, and distinguishing those debates from the ZOG narrative is analytically essential. AIPAC, or the American Israel Public Affairs Committee, is an influential pro-Israel lobbying group with interests that often align with the Israeli government's. The question of whether AIPAC should have to register as a foreign agent under FARA has been a point of contention for years. According to Matthew Sanderson, a lawyer specializing in advising clients on FARA, the law is clear that AIPAC does not have to register as a foreign agent — while the law does have some grey areas, he said, this is not one of them. The legal basis for this finding is that FARA defines a foreign agent as someone who acts "at the order, request, or under the direction or control" of a foreign government; when determining if an organization falls under that definition, the government considers factors like foreign funding, contractual ties to foreign governments, and foreign control of the organization's board. AIPAC is not a foreign government instrument — it is funded by private American donors trying to make a change in American policy. Critically, the AIPAC-as-ZOG argument requires that AIPAC's existence *proves* a covert ethnic control structure. But there are hundreds of foreign-interest lobbying organizations in Washington — including those representing Saudi Arabia, Taiwan, Turkey, and dozens more — registered or otherwise. The existence of a pro-Israel lobby is consistent with the normal pluralist competition of interests that characterizes all American foreign policy, not evidence of singular ethnic domination. Furthermore, the U.S.-Israel relationship has been marked by repeated, documented public conflicts: Netanyahu has insisted that the IDF would proceed to attack Gaza's Rafah despite clear U.S. warnings, declaring that Israel is "not a banana republic." The U.S.-Israeli alliance has weathered many disagreements over the decades. A government that was actually controlled by Israeli interests would not need to be argued with and defied by the Israeli prime minister.

The banking and "ZioCorp" variants of the theory commit the same logical error at greater scale. They identify prominent Jewish individuals in finance — which reflects the documented historical reality that Jewish people in America, after overcoming extensive discrimination, became represented in fields like law, medicine, academia, and finance at rates above their share of the population — and then assert that this representation constitutes coordinated ethnic control. This is the base-rate fallacy applied to ethnicity. Jewish Americans constitute roughly 2% of the U.S. population. When individuals from any group are represented in elite positions above that baseline, the ZOG framework interprets it not as the outcome of educational attainment, historical circumstance, or meritocratic competition, but as evidence of a conspiracy. This makes the theory unfalsifiable by design: any Jewish success confirms control, any Jewish absence is evidence of concealment. The "ZioCorp" rebranding as a transnational structure operating "above nation-states" simply makes the theory more elastic — it can absorb any counterevidence by relocating the alleged control to a layer that is, by definition, unobservable. That is not an empirical upgrade; it is the architecture of an unfalsifiable belief system.

There is a legitimate kernel of concern that this theory parasitizes. Foreign lobbying in American politics is a genuine transparency problem — not unique to pro-Israel groups but real across the board. The Foreign Agents Registration Act was passed in 1938 in direct response to Nazi Germany's propaganda operations; its premise was that if you are acting on behalf of a foreign government to influence American policy, the American public has a right to know. Questions about whether FARA's current structure adequately covers sophisticated domestic lobbying organizations that advance foreign-state interests — questions raised by legal scholars, journalists, and legislators across the political spectrum — are legitimate and important. Similarly, the very real concentration of corporate power in media is worth scrutinizing. What the ZOG framework does is take these valid governance concerns and detach them from their actual institutional causes — corporate consolidation, campaign finance law, lobbying industry structures — and redirect them toward an ethnic scapegoat. It converts a policy problem that has structural solutions into a racial conspiracy that demands only one solution: the removal of Jewish influence. That substitution is not an analytic refinement. It is the mechanism by which legitimate grievance becomes weaponized prejudice.

The documented human cost of this narrative is substantial and specific. William Luther Pierce, who popularized much of the ZOG framework, wrote *The Turner Diaries* in 1978 — a novel depicting a violent white supremacist revolution against a Jewish-controlled U.S. government — which served as an operational blueprint for real-world terrorists, including Timothy McVeigh, who carried pages of the novel during the 1995 Oklahoma City bombing that killed 168 people. The FBI introduced the novel in trial as the "bible of the racist right" to help explain the perpetrators' motive for the attack on the Murrah Federal Building. The theory has resulted in hateful slogans and hashtags like "Smash ZOG," "Kill ZOG," and "Death to ZOG." The theme has received a boost amid surging antisemitism in the wake of the October 7, 2023 Hamas massacres in Israel and the subsequent war in Gaza. The theory's current rebranding as "ZioCorp" — making it sound more like geopolitical analysis than racial hatred — does not alter its function: it is a violence-adjacent ideology that has already moved from pamphlet to bomb and will do so again if its audience grows. Analysts, journalists, and content moderators should recognize the continuity between the 1976 neo-Nazi original and its updated corporate vocabulary. The premise is identical. The history is not metaphorical.
