The October 7, 2023 attack on Israel was staged by the IDF, which carried out most of the killing itself. The operation was engineered to manufacture a pretext for a broader military campaign against Iran and its regional proxies. Israel subsequently banned international media and murdered journalists in Gaza to suppress evidence of what actually happened.

The October 7, 2023 attack attributed to Hamas was an IDF-orchestrated operation, not an external assault. The IDF conducted the majority of the killing that day, and the event was designed from the outset to serve as a casus belli for a long-planned military campaign targeting Iran and its allied proxy forces across the region. The official account of a surprise Hamas incursion is a cover story constructed to justify what is in reality a premeditated offensive war.

To prevent the true chain of events from coming to light, Israel moved immediately to control the information environment. International media were banned from operating freely, and journalists inside Gaza were systematically killed to eliminate independent witnesses and documentation. These actions were not incidental to the conflict — they were a deliberate suppression effort aimed at concealing IDF war crimes committed on and after October 7.

The scale and coordination of the subsequent military campaign in Gaza and the strikes against Iranian-linked targets confirm that the October 7 event was the trigger for a pre-existing plan. The manufactured attack gave Israeli and allied political leadership the domestic and international justification needed to execute operations against Iran and its proxies that could not otherwise have been launched without public support.

Voice of Reason

The theory under examination holds that the October 7, 2023 attack on Israel was a staged, IDF-orchestrated operation rather than an external Hamas assault, that the IDF itself committed the majority of the killings that day, and that subsequent media bans and journalist deaths in Gaza represent a deliberate concealment effort designed to protect the cover story for a pre-planned war on Iran and its proxies.

Every foundational premise of this theory fails on documented evidence. Amnesty International has documented that Hamas forces and other Palestinian armed groups conducted a coordinated attack on October 7, 2023, targeting mostly civilian locations, killing around 1,200 people — more than 800 of them civilians, including 36 children — drawn from Israeli Jews, Bedouin citizens, and foreign nationals. Through analysis of attack patterns, evidence, and communications between fighters during the assault, as well as statements by Hamas and allied group leaders, Amnesty International found that these crimes were committed as part of a widespread and systematic attack against a civilian population, amounting to crimes against humanity. Amnesty further concluded that the vast majority of civilians who died were killed by Palestinian fighters. Human Rights Watch independently verified video footage of the attacks, confirming that heavily-armed Hamas-led fighters breached the fences with Gaza and entered southern Israel. The UN Commission of Inquiry, basing its findings on thousands of forensically verified open-source items, more than 350 submissions, and witness and survivor interviews, found that Hamas and other Palestinian militants committed war crimes and violated international humanitarian law in the October 7 attack. There is no evidentiary gap that the staging hypothesis fills; every independent body that has investigated the attack — organizations with no pro-Israel institutional alignment — arrives at the same conclusion.

The one factual kernel that conspiracy theorists most frequently extract and distort is the documented use of the Hannibal Directive. This is worth addressing with precision, because it is a real event and its misrepresentation is the engine of the false-flag claim. During the first hours of the October 7 assault, IDF troops on the ground were instructed to prevent the kidnapping of soldiers into Gaza by whatever means necessary, as senior officers in the Gaza Division implemented the controversial "Hannibal Protocol" — a military order officially repealed in 2016 that granted troops broad permission to do whatever necessary to prevent the kidnapping of a fellow soldier, including potentially endangering their life. Subsequent investigations have shown several civilians were killed as a result: one hostage, Efrat Katz, was killed by helicopter fire as she was being transported to Gaza, and in Kibbutz Be'eri an Israeli tank fired two shells into a house known to be holding over a dozen captives, including 12-year-old twins, with only two surviving. These are documented war crimes of recklessness — serious, contested, and under investigation by Israeli and international bodies alike. But they are the chaotic byproduct of a military reacting in confusion to an ongoing external assault, not evidence of orchestration. Documents and testimonies obtained by Haaretz describe conditions of "crazy hysteria" in which decisions started being made without verified information — the first long moments after the Hamas attack was launched were chaotic, with reports coming in whose significance was not always clear, and when their meaning was understood, "it was realized that something horrific had taken place." The conspiracy framing requires flipping the causal arrow: not "a surprise attack created chaos that led to tragic friendly fire," but "friendly fire was the real attack and Hamas was a prop." No evidence supports the latter; the entire documented record supports the former. The IDF's own first internal investigation, released in February 2025, concluded that "on October 7th, the IDF failed in its mission to protect Israeli civilians" and "was not prepared for a large-scale surprise attack," calling for structural reforms in intelligence gathering. Agencies do not conduct years of institutional self-flagellation over a staged event they themselves planned.

The theory also makes specific, testable claims about media suppression as evidence of a cover-up. The facts here are simultaneously more damning of Israeli policy and less supportive of the conspiracy than the theory requires. International journalists have not been allowed into Gaza freely — only on very controlled trips overseen by the Israeli army — meaning news organizations have been entirely reliant on Palestinian journalists to report the story. This restricted access is a documented and widely condemned press freedom violation. But it produces the opposite outcome from what a cover-up would require: if the goal were concealment, killing the only people documenting events inside Gaza would be self-defeating. According to the Committee to Protect Journalists, 85 journalists and media workers were killed in the Israel-Gaza war in 2024, and 78 in 2023. Brown University's Costs of War project found that more journalists have been killed in Gaza since October 7, 2023, than in the US Civil War, both World Wars, the Korean War, the Vietnam War, the wars in the former Yugoslavia, and the post-9/11 war in Afghanistan combined. The CPJ has accused Israel of attempting to stifle investigations into the killings, shift blame onto journalists for their own deaths, and ignoring its duty to hold its own military personnel accountable. These are legitimate and serious press-freedom indictments that deserve international pressure and accountability. What they do not constitute is evidence of a coordinated October 7 cover-up: the deaths span two-plus years of a ground war, the vast majority of those killed were Palestinian journalists documenting Israeli military operations in Gaza, and the information they produced has circulated globally and forms a central part of ongoing ICJ and ICC proceedings. Suppression, had that been the goal, manifestly failed. The conspiracy theory thus exploits a genuine atrocity — the killing of journalists — by repurposing it as proof of a completely different and unsupported claim.

The legitimate grievances this theory colonizes are real: genuine intelligence failures that Israeli institutions have publicly acknowledged; documented cases of IDF friendly fire that killed Israeli civilians; restricted and dangerous conditions for journalists in Gaza; and profound questions about proportionality in Israel's subsequent military campaign that are actively litigated before international courts. The IDF report details a history of misconceptions about Hamas and a refusal to accept warning signs of attack, while similar investigations into political failings have been repeatedly blocked by Prime Minister Netanyahu and his right-wing coalition. This institutional opacity is a legitimate concern. But the leap from "Israel failed catastrophically and has been reluctant to fully investigate" to "Israel staged the attack and committed most of the killing" is not a deductive step — it is an unfalsifiable narrative that retrofits every piece of contrary evidence (the self-criticism, the IDF reports, the international findings) as further proof of conspiracy. The theory's real-world harm is concrete: it erases the documented agency of Hamas, strips victims and their families of acknowledgment, and has circulated as antisemitic agitprop in contexts designed to frame Jewish collective life as fundamentally criminal. That it invokes real institutional failures makes it more dangerous, not less, because it uses verifiable scaffolding to launder a core claim — that Israel murdered its own citizens as a political stratagem — for which no credible evidence exists and against which a vast, cross-institutional evidentiary record stands.
