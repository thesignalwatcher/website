Each of those points is asserted, not demonstrated.

The structure here is the tell: any detail that fits gets treated as confirmation, and any missing evidence gets folded in as proof of the cover-up. A calm photographer could reflect training, distance, or shock. Someone laughing after a tense moment could be nervous relief. Neither requires foreknowledge — but once you assume a hidden operation, both seem to snap into place. That feeling of pieces clicking together is not the same as those pieces being real. A claim this large — coordinated actors, suppressed media, repositioned security, a complicit hotel — would require an enormous number of people to stay silent. Before accepting the machine, it is worth asking: what single piece of verifiable evidence would you need to see, and where would you go to find it?
