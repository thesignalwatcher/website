The empirical problems are fundamental and falsifiable. Hantaviruses are not new pathogens—they are a well-documented family of viruses carried by rodents, first identified in the 1950s during the Korean War and named after the Hantaan River. Hantavirus Pulmonary Syndrome has been recognized in the United States since 1993, with the CDC tracking 20-40 cases annually, primarily in rural areas where humans encounter infected rodent droppings or urine. The epidemiology, geographic distribution, and transmission patterns have remained consistent for decades and are incompatible with engineered release or person-to-person pandemic spread. Hantaviruses do not transmit efficiently between humans—nearly all cases result from direct rodent contact, which is why outbreaks are rare, geographically isolated, and do not produce exponential case growth. The claim of a cruise ship outbreak serving as a pandemic launch mechanism is particularly incoherent given this transmission biology; no credible reports of such an outbreak exist, and hantavirus transmission in a cruise ship environment (absent a rodent infestation) is biologically implausible.
